# axm-compute-substrate

AXM research into the **history, physics, architecture and future substrates of computation**.

The repo is intentionally broader than semiconductor design.

Modern silicon is one powerful implementation family. It is not the definition or historical beginning of computation.

## Current research roots

- [COMPUTE_LINEAGE.md](COMPUTE_LINEAGE.md) — evidence-backed lineage extending the research before the 1837 Analytical Engine formal root, including ancient physical calculation, mechanical/analog computation, early mechanical arithmetic, punched machine control and Babbage.
- [PHYSICAL_COMPUTE_BOUNDARY.md](PHYSICAL_COMPUTE_BOUNDARY.md) — physical constraints future software-generated compute must eventually model: thermodynamics, propagation, topology, synchronization, noise, geometry, heat, memory, fabrication, reliability and reversibility.
- [LICENSE_BOUNDARY.md](LICENSE_BOUNDARY.md) — AXM/third-party licensing boundary.

## Relationship to the active Compute Substrate Lab

AXM also has an executable experimental Compute Substrate Lab inside the collaboration platform.

That runtime currently protects **1837** as a provisional working root specifically for:

FORMAL_PROGRAMMABLE_GENERAL_PURPOSE_MACHINE_LINEAGE

This repo does not silently rewrite that narrower root.

Instead, it stores the broader research that explains what came before 1837 and what physical/substrate lessons should feed future architecture work.

The distinction matters:

~~~text
broader embodied-computation history
        |
        +-- manual / symbolic physical state
        +-- analog / mechanical calculation
        +-- arithmetic automation
        +-- encoded machine control
        +-- programmable general-purpose architecture
        +-- electromechanical / electronic / optical / other later branches
~~~

## North-star question

> What is computation when we stop assuming "CPU + RAM + silicon chip" is the only valid physical shape?

Research can include:

- historical compute mechanisms;
- mechanical and analog computation;
- electronic and semiconductor architecture;
- optical/photonics;
- fluidic and mechanical logic;
- reversible/adiabatic computation;
- neuromorphic and memristive systems;
- biological/chemical computation;
- quantum computation;
- heterogeneous/hybrid architectures;
- machine-designed physical compute;
- personal/custom chips and other local compute structures.

## Research discipline

1. **History is evidence-backed.** Do not invent a clean lineage where history is ambiguous.
2. **"First" claims are scoped.** First calculating aid, first punch-card control and first programmable general-purpose design are different claims.
3. **Physics survives abstraction.** Software may explore freely, but physical candidates must eventually state energy, timing, geometry, noise, fabrication and recovery assumptions.
4. **Old ideas are not automatically obsolete.** Ask why they lost, whether the original constraint still exists, and what modern materials/simulation/automation change.
5. **No automatic canon.** Research findings are candidates until evidence and use justify stronger status.

## Intended future split

Once this foundation is sufficiently grounded, a later dedicated repo can focus on **personal compute structures / custom chip and non-chip design**.

That future repo should consume:

- historical primitives from COMPUTE_LINEAGE.md;
- physical constraints from PHYSICAL_COMPUTE_BOUNDARY.md;
- modern semiconductor/chip design lessons;
- alternative-substrate research;
- simulation and fabrication constraints.

The aim is not to recreate historical machines.

The aim is to recover useful computational principles and make them available to modern automated design.
